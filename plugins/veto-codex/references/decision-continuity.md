# Decision and correction continuity

Use this reference for a request to **record why we chose this**, **revisit the
original reasoning**, or **fix a mistake we keep making**. It adapts the useful
recordkeeping and complete-job ideas from the separate `record-the-decision` and
`use-ai-well` utilities to Veto's existing direct-ownership workflow. It does not
replace either personal utility, create a new queue, or turn a recommendation
into permission.

## Keep one record for one choice

First locate the existing authoritative work or decision record for the same
choice. Search the current task's accepted record and the established decision
location before creating anything. A small Veto choice can use
`templates/decision-card.md`; a substantial business or investment memo remains
in the separate decision utility's scope.

Record these fields in plain language:

- **Status:** Proposed, Decided, Deferred or Superseded.
- **Owner and date:** the person or role with authority, decision date if known,
  and recording date when they differ.
- **Scope and source:** what the choice covers, what it excludes, and the exact
  conversation, artifact or source that supplied the decision.
- **Choice and reasoning at the time:** the selected route, material observed
  evidence, interpretation, strongest alternative and tradeoff. Mark inference,
  reported claims and unknowns instead of filling gaps.
- **Uncertainty and reopening:** what remains unknown, the next permitted
  commitment and the observable change that would justify revisiting it.

Keep a proposed recommendation visibly **Proposed** until the actual decision
owner chooses. An approved decision governs its stated scope; it does not make
an assumption true, approve a separate send/payment/release, or silently widen
the product. Append later amendments and reviews beneath the original entry so
the earlier reasoning remains recoverable.

## Turn a repeated mistake into a bounded correction

When a later task reports the same avoidable failure, do not merely add a
reminder. Capture one correction card in the existing work record with:

1. **Observed failure and scope:** the exact case, candidate or handoff, what
   happened, and what remains reported or unknown.
2. **Cause and consequence:** the earliest missing context, tool, check,
   ownership join or permission boundary that explains the failure. Separate a
   hypothesis from the evidence supporting it.
3. **Smallest repair:** the one existing skill, reference, helper or workflow
   change that is authorized to address that cause. Keep unrelated product work,
   new services and broad policy changes out.
4. **Regression case:** a positive control that should still succeed, the failure
   case that should now be caught or repaired, and the exact artifact/state that
   demonstrates the difference.
5. **Later retrieval check:** the record path or stable topic, the next
   applicable task, and the observed result. A later task must reopen the saved
   correction and apply it; a claim that it will be remembered is not a check.

If evidence defeats the diagnosis, narrow or withdraw it and preserve the
failed attempt. Do not rewrite the original decision to make the correction look
obvious. If the repair would alter authority, accepted scope, external effects
or an irreversible safeguard, stop at the boundary and name the owner/action
needed.

## Finish and report

Read the saved record back. Return its path, status, owner, scope, source,
reopening trigger and the next permitted action. State whether the correction
was proposed, applied, verified later, or remains untested. If no durable
writing location is available, return a complete draft and say that retrieval
has not been established. Keep one work record and one accountable owner; the
founder is not a message relay for routine handoffs.

This procedure improves continuity only when a later task actually retrieves and
uses the record. A Markdown file, hash, helper pass or additional skill read is
evidence of preparation, not proof of better work or customer value.
